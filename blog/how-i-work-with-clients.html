I don’t start with a website. I start with you.

People first. Websites second. Here’s exactly what happens when you work with me.

By Sarah J Hill21 November 20265 min read

I feel your pain, and I solve it. Most web designers ask what colour you want. I ask what you want to be celebrating a year from now. Then I find out what your business is really worth, dig out the problems you didn’t know were costing you, and tell you straight what will fix them. The website comes after that. Not before.

What does “human-centric” actually mean?

It means I design around people, not pages. You, and the people you serve. Before I think about a single screen I want to know who’ll be using it, what kind of day they’re having, and what they need to feel to trust you.

A nervous first-time customer. A volunteer on their phone between shifts. Someone who’s struggling and needs help in two taps, not ten. Someone using a screen reader, or reading in their second language. If the site doesn’t work for them, it doesn’t work. Full stop.

And it means treating you like a person, not a ticket number. I listen more than I talk, I explain things in plain English, and you always know what’s happening next.

Why do I get it?

Because I’ve felt it. I know what it’s like to be overlooked, talked over and underestimated before you’ve said a word. I’m a woman who taught herself to code in an industry that doesn’t look like me. So when you tell me people don’t take you seriously, I don’t nod politely. I feel it.

And then I do something about it. I feel your pain, and I solve it. That’s the whole job.

Why don’t I just start building?

Because a website built on guesses is a guess with a price tag. If I don’t know who your customers are, what they doubt about you and what you want them to do, I’m decorating. Not solving.

So before anyone talks templates, fonts or plugins, we talk about you.

How do I get to know your full potential?

It starts with ten minutes of honest answers. My Light the fire form asks what your business does, who it’s for, what you want your site to do, and the big one:

“Picture us celebrating a year after launch. What has your website done for you?”

That one question tells me more than any brief. It shows me what success actually means to you, in your words, not mine.

Then I do something most people don’t. I sleep on it. I read your answers, wait a day, and read them again. You get a considered reply within two working days, not a rushed sales pitch the same afternoon.

What happens on the call?

About 45 minutes, on a video call or the phone. I’ll have done my homework first: your business, your competitors, and a proper check of your current site’s speed, search visibility and gaps. So we don’t waste the call on the basics.

Then I ask questions. Lots of them. The most useful one is the simplest:

  • “Why do you need a new website?”
  • Then: “Why else?” And again. Until you run out.
  • Then: “If you could only pick one, which matters most? Why?”

That’s where the real brief lives. The first answer is usually “it looks dated”. The fourth or fifth is the one that matters: “people don’t take us seriously”, “I’m losing jobs to bigger firms”, “I want to grow into a second city”.

And I don’t talk code. You’ll never hear me bang on about plugins. It’s your goals, your customers and your money we’re talking about.

How do I find problems you don’t know you’ve got?

By looking where nobody else looks. These are the ones I find again and again, and almost nobody knew they had them:

  • A contact form emailing nobody. Sometimes for months. Every enquiry, gone.
  • A site that crawls on a phone. It looks fine on your laptop at home. Your customers are on mobile data at a bus stop.
  • Invisible on Google for your own town. You rank for your business name, but not for “electrician in Cardiff”, which is what people actually type.
  • No proof up top. Your best reviews are buried three clicks deep, so new visitors never see them.
  • Words that undersell you. “Just a small business”, “we’ll try our best”. You’re better than that, so your site should say so.
  • Admin eating your evenings. Bookings by text, a rota on a spreadsheet, donations by bank transfer. Often the biggest win isn’t a prettier homepage at all. It’s a simple tool that gives you your hours back.

That last one is why I’m a developer as well as a designer. When the real problem is a process, I can build the thing that fixes it: online bookings, volunteer sign-ups, donation pages, client portals.

What does consultation with me actually look like?

Honest. Within 24 hours of our call you get a written recap of everything I heard: what your business needs, what your customers need, what success looks like, and the budget you’ve set aside. You check it, you correct anything I got wrong, and only then do I write a proposal.

The proposal is built from your own words. No surprises, no jargon, and a fixed price agreed before anything starts.

And if what you need isn’t what you asked for, I’ll tell you. If a cheaper first step will get you further, I’ll say so. If I’m not the right person for the job, I’ll say that too and point you to someone who is. That’s what a consultant is for.

What happens once we start?

You get your own private project page. Within two working days of saying yes, you’ll have a plan with real dates on it, and you can watch every task tick off as I go. You will never have to type “any update?” See the whole process.

How do I make sure real people can use it?

  • Phone first. That’s where most people meet you, so that’s where I start.
  • Accessible by default. Readable text, strong contrast, big tap targets, works with a keyboard and a screen reader. Not an extra. The baseline.
  • Plain words. If your nan can’t follow it, it gets rewritten.
  • Tested on real people. I watch someone try to book, donate or get in touch, and fix whatever trips them up.
  • Built for you to run. You can change your own words and photos without ringing me.

Who is this for?

Women in business, charities, community groups and anyone who’s tired of being underestimated. People who know what they want, mean what they say, and are ready to take their business seriously. If that’s you, we’ll get on.
