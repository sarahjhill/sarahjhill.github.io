# Storyboard — sarahjhill.com

Eleven scenes. Every one has a job, and if it isn't doing it, it comes out.

The whole page is one argument: *they decide in three seconds → here's what that
costs you → here's proof → here's me → here's the fix.*

---

## Scene 01 · The Judgement
**Hero · 0:00–0:03 · dark**

**On screen.** A counter at 100px reading `3`. The headline sits behind heavy
blur, unreadable. A flame line begins crossing the top of the window.

**Copy.** *That's how long they take to write you off.*

**Motion.** The counter ticks 3 → 2 → 1 over exactly three seconds. At zero the
blur snaps to nothing, the label changes to **"they have decided"**, and the
sub-line and buttons rise. The flame line then becomes the scroll indicator.

**The job.** Make the visitor *feel* the three seconds instead of reading about
them. The blur is the point — for three seconds they cannot read the headline,
which is exactly what happens to the woman this site is for.

**Exit.** "Try it on yourself" → Scene 03. Scroll cue bobs.

---

## Scene 02 · The Recognition
**Who it's for · cream**

**On screen.** Three numbered lines, thin rules between, generous space.

**Copy.**
> 01 She arrives with the tools. — *"Is your husband parking the van?"*
> 02 Twenty years qualified. — *"Are you on reception, love?"*
> 03 Best on the list. — *Nobody scrolled that far.*

**Motion.** Each line rises 26px as it enters, staggered 70ms. Hovering nudges
a line 10px right.

**The job.** One of these has happened to her. This is where she stops reading
a website and starts reading *her own life*.

**Exit.** Straight into the test.

---

## Scene 03 · The Proof
**The 3-second test · black · INTERACTIVE**

**On screen.** Two electrician cards. **A** is bare — a name, a trade, one grey
line. **B** has a face, 4.9 from 47 reviews, 18 years qualified, NICEIC, £2m
insured. A bar above them starts draining the moment the section is 45% visible.

**Copy.** *Three seconds. Who do you ring?*

**Motion.** Three-second bar. Click either card and it locks in amber; the
verdict slides up.

**The three endings.**
- **Picks B** → *"Same electrician. Both of them."* Nothing about her skill
  changed. B just put the proof where the doubt was.
- **Picks A** → *"Brave. Almost nobody does."* Choosing her was a gamble, and
  most people won't gamble with their kitchen wiring.
- **Runs out of time** → *"You didn't ring anyone. Which is what most of them
  do."* They don't pick wrong — they go back to Google and you never hear about it.

**The job.** The single most important scene. Every other section *tells*;
this one makes the argument happen to the reader. The timeout ending is the
best one — it is the real business problem, demonstrated.

**Exit.** Hard cut to flame.

## Scene 04 · The Promise
**Manifesto · full-bleed flame**

**Copy.**
> I say the thing everyone else softens.
> Your website should too.

**Motion.** Two lines, rising, staggered.

**The job.** A breath after the test, and the moment the site stops describing
a service and states a character. Colour does the work — no other section is
this loud.

---

## Scene 05 · The Person
**About · cream**

**On screen.** Portrait left, greyscale, with a flame frame offset behind it.
A flame tag: *Sarah J Hill · Cardiff & Birmingham*. Story right.

**Copy.** *I've been the one they underestimated.* Career change, Code
Institute, community kitchens and student groups — closing on **"I don't do
quiet and I don't do jargon. If your idea won't work I'll say so on the first
call. If it's brilliant I'll be completely unbearable about it."**

**Motion.** The portrait goes full colour on hover.

**The job.** Credibility by kinship. She isn't buying a service, she's backing
someone who has been in the same room. The greyscale-to-colour is the whole
positioning in one interaction.

---

## Scene 06 · The Offer
**What you get · sand**

**On screen.** Five rows: title and a plain sentence left, a flame-ruled
**YOU GET** payoff right.

**The job.** Scannable in ten seconds, readable in two minutes. Every row has a
payoff so nothing is a feature — it is all consequence.

---

## Scene 07 · The Evidence
**Work · dark**

**On screen.** Four tiles: Cardiff Community Meals, Many Voices, Muslimah Web
Design, the Dragon Fire Process. Images scale 6% on hover.

**The job.** She has just been told to lead with proof. This is the site doing
what it sells. Real, live, clickable.

---

## Scene 08 · The Product
**Dragon Fire Process · sand**

**On screen.** Four counters — 12 / 69 / 72 / 5 — animating from zero on entry.
Then rows split *for the client* and *for the studio*, chips, two buttons.

**Copy.** *Nobody should have to type "any update?"*

**The job.** The second revenue stream. Reassurance for clients, a licence for
other studios, and quiet proof of how seriously the work is taken.

---

## Scene 09 · The Voices
**Testimonials · cream**

**Copy.** *Ask the people who paid me.* Three real quotes.

**The job.** Short and unglamorous on purpose. It sits between the product and
the offer so the free thing that follows feels earned.

---

## Scene 10 · The Hook
**Doubt Audit · dark**

**Copy.** *How many decide against you before they ring?* Then the honesty
rows — *what I won't do* ("your website is losing you £47,000 a year" — bin it)
and *and if it's fine?*

**The job.** A free, low-risk first step, pitched at the fear rather than the
service. Refusing to invent a number is what makes the rest believable.

---

## Scene 11 · The Ask
**Close · black**

**Copy.** *Let's get you taken seriously.* Two working days, an honest answer,
even if it's *"not me — try her instead"*.

**The job.** Ends on the promise the site opened with. Turning work away in the
final line is the strongest trust signal on the page.

---

## Rules

1. **Every animation carries meaning.** The countdown is the argument, the blur
   is the judgement, the greyscale-to-colour is being seen. Nothing moves to
   look busy.
2. **Every section has one job.** If it can't be named in a sentence, cut it.
3. **Dark → light → flame.** Rhythm carries the reader; flame is spent sparingly
   so it still lands.
4. **Reduce motion gets the finished state.** No countdown, no reveals — the
   argument survives in the words alone.
5. **Say it, then prove it.** Scene 06 promises proof-first design; scene 07 is
   the site doing it.
