# Positioning — Sarah J Hill

The document this site is built from. If a change to the site doesn't agree with
this file, one of the two is wrong.

---

## The insight

Muslim women in business and women in scarce trades are not two audiences.
They are one.

**Both get underestimated before they open their mouth.**

A woman electrician turns up and gets asked when her husband is coming.
A woman pitches for the contract and watches someone decide about her in the
first three seconds.

Same wound, different room. Neither has a skills problem. They have a
**being-believed** problem — and that is not a web design problem, which is
exactly why every web designer misses it.

> Her website is the only room she controls the door to.

---

## The line

**Taken seriously before you say a word.**

Seven words. Says who it's for, what it does, and why it matters. No other web
designer in Britain can say it without sounding borrowed — because it comes from
my own life. A woman learning to code in an industry that doesn't look like me.
I am the customer.

---

## Voice

Comedy, passion and strength — while staying professional and serious.

**Rules:**

- **Talk TO her, never ABOUT her.** The site is addressed to one person, so it is
  second person throughout: *you*, *your*, *you arrive*. Never *she*, *her* or
  *a woman who*. The only exception is a testimonial, where someone else is
  quoted speaking about Sarah.
- Say the thing everyone else softens.
- Short words. Short lines. Nothing anyone has to read twice.
- Dry, not zany. The joke is the truth said plainly.
- Never punch at the reader. Punch at what's stacked against them.
- Every claim has to survive being read out loud in front of the person it's about.

**On brand:**

- "You arrive with the tools." / "Is your husband parking the van?"
- "Not a stock photo of a man in a hard hat looking pleased with a clipboard."
- "There's no missed-call list for 'looked a bit unconvincing'."
- "Bullshit 0%."

**Off brand:**

- "Elite solutions." "Strategic intent." "Take your business to the next level."
- Anything that could appear on ten thousand other agency sites.

## Who it's for

| Audience | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Women in scarce trades** | Electricians, plumbers, joiners, plasterers, mechanics. Roughly 1 in 50 UK tradespeople are women. Almost none have a decent website — most run off a Facebook page. Underserved, they talk to each other constantly, and they buy on trust. |
| **Muslim women in business** | Muslimah Web Design, Muslim Bookers, Salafi Bookshop, Islam in Cardiff, Sakeenah Education are already built. That is a portfolio most designers could never assemble, and standing that cannot be copied. |

Both answer the same question — *will they take me seriously?* — so one message
serves both and neither dilutes the other.

---

## The three things that make it un-copyable

**1. The Doubt Audit.** Everyone offers a free website audit. Mine answers a
different question: *how many customers doubt you before they ever ring?* Same
work, entirely different promise, and it is the exact fear the audience wakes
up with.

**2. Proof-first design.** A standard site leads with a pretty hero. Mine leads
with evidence — qualifications, insurance, reviews, real photographs of her
doing the work. Every decision aimed at killing the doubt in the first three
seconds.

**3. The directory.** A public, searchable list of women in the trades,
free to join and free to search. The moment it exists I stop being a supplier
and become the hub. Clients join something instead of buying something. It is
also a local news story that isn't about web design at all.

## The product

**The SJH Process** — 12 phases, 69 guided tasks, 72 templates, 5 client forms.

Sold on one promise: *nobody should have to type "any update?"*.
The industry standard is three weeks of silence and a hopeful email. This is
the opposite. Clients sign in and watch it happen.

Two markets: clients (reassurance) and other studios (licence it).

  Birmingham. Pick one or claim both deliberately.
